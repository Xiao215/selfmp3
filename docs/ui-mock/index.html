2 · Phone

1 · Getting in

First launch shows tag tiles with invented everyday names, because nobody else's covers are right for a stranger. A device that has signed in before shows the last covers it kept. One button, Continue with Google. There is no sign-in screen inside the app, and Sign out comes back here.

First sync: playing can start while covers arrive. 'Keep every song on this phone' is off by default.

2 · Home, tags and artists

Home is the landing page, in this order: greeting in the serif, search field, four tag tiles, 'All 8 tags', recently played. No Shuffle all. The avatar opens You, + opens Import. On Sunday one card sits under the greeting and opens the month page; it is not there the rest of the week.

All tags: most played first; songs with no tag yet are a card at the top, never a page of their own. Hold a tag to rename, recolour or delete it.

A tag page is lit by its covers: Play, Shuffle, Add. Add opens the sheet that combines tags and artists: search, the chosen ones on one row that scrolls sideways, Tags and Artists lists, and the count only on the button. An artist page is the same page with a figure where the dot would be.

3 · Library, a song, playlists

One row component everywhere: cover, title, artist with the on-device mark, time, ⋯. Library and Search are the only lists that show tags on rows: two, then a count.

Selecting: the action bar sits at the bottom on a phone. The ⋯ menu has no Play next and no Select.

Tapping a title opens the song's own page: cover, tags, Play, Lyrics, then plays, first heard, a small history, and songs that sound like it.

Playlists sort by last played. No pins, no empty playlists, no download button on the page.

4 · Search

One Search page. Home's field, the search circle in the bar and Library's field all open it; the door only sets the scope it starts on. Before typing it shows your tags and what you played last. There is no search history.

5 · Playing

The mini player is a floating card above the bar with a progress wash and a queue button; tapping it opens Now Playing.

Now Playing: no selector at the top. ⓘ opens the song's page. The foot is Lyrics · Sleep · queue. Tags wrap quietly under the artist. Lyrics is a swipe or the pill and opens the lyrics-only page. A song without lyrics shows Horizon or Ripples instead, coloured from its cover.

Up next is a sheet that covers the mini player and the bar. The playing song is on top with an equaliser. Hold to move, swipe left to remove; no grips, no ×. Played songs sink to the end, greyed.

Lock screen: the system player; no Loved button and no add-tag button. Widget: four tag tiles, tap to play, and what is playing.

6 · Import

Paste a link, or share one to the app from YouTube. 'Look it up' opens the review list. Every song is coming in unless it is left out: no checkmarks. Tap a song and its row opens with Title and Artist fields and a waveform you can drag to listen; there is no album field. Swipe left to leave a song out; it stays in the list, dimmed and struck through. A song you already have says so and is skipped. Importing can add a tag; it never offers a playlist.

7 · You, stats, settings

You is behind the avatar on Home: the person, this month as one card, then Import, Report and Settings as rows.

Stats is a page of cards: Listened, Peak hour, Streak, then one ranked module that switches between Songs, Artists and Tags.

Report opens the month as a page, with five looks to pick from: Paper, Receipt, Wall, Calendar, Words. It is an image to keep or share.

Settings: accent swatches plus a hue slider; storage lives under 'On this phone'.

3 · Computer and web

1 · Getting in

Same two steps as the phone. 'Keep every song on this computer' is on by default here. The web app is this same layout in a tab: no menu bar, no ⌘K, Space for play and pause, and no on-device marks because a browser streams.

C01 · Welcome C02 · First sync

2 · Browsing

Sidebar, top to bottom: Search (⌘K), Home, Library, Import, then Playlists and Tags as sections whose headers open the full list and whose rows are the most recent few, then your name, then Settings last.

Home has a large search field. The field in a list header and ⌘K are the same search, drawn as a palette over the page.

A tag, a playlist and an artist are one page layout. Tags show on rows in Library and Search only.

The player bar is absent while nothing is loaded.

3 · Playing

Player bar: cover and title, transport, speed, volume, Up next, devices, with the same progress wash as the phone's mini player.

Now Playing has Lyrics (or Visual) and About; Up next is not a tab there because the rail already exists.

Up next is a rail beside the list. Drag to reorder. Drag a row out of the rail to remove it, with Undo; Delete on a focused row and the right-click menu do the same and draw nothing.

4 · Import, stats, settings

Import review: rows stay aligned in one grid. Under the pointer a row shows play over its cover and 'Leave out' at its end. The playing row turns its title and artist into fields, and a bar with a draggable waveform opens directly under that row, as wide as the title and artist columns.

Stats is the same cards as the phone, wider. The month as a page is a newspaper front page here.

4 · iPad

1 · Full screen, portrait

An iPad in portrait is 834 wide: past the 820 breakpoint, so it gets the computer's layout, with a finger. Sidebar, player bar and the rail as on a computer, under the status bar and above the home indicator (the bar grows by the bottom inset).

Each page gives way at that width rather than squeezing: Welcome's text half takes a 48 margin and the tiles two abreast; Home's This week goes under the tiles, its two cards side by side, and the tiles take the page's whole width; a tag page's buttons go under its name; a row drops the album column. Up next lies over the page with a shadow instead of taking a column. The bar keeps the song at 220 and loses Like and Tags (they are on Now Playing and in the song menu); volume is the speaker alone.

Now Playing stacks: cover beside the title, then Lyrics or Visual below, the close button under the status bar.

Words name the device: 'on this iPad', 'tells this iPad where it is'.

2 · Landscape, and beside other apps

Landscape is 1194: the computer's layout nearly as drawn. This week is beside the tiles, the rail beside the page, the bar in full with Like, Tags and a volume slider.

Split View and Slide Over give the app a window narrower than 820, and it becomes the phone: tabs, the mini player, sheets. At 678 and 507 that is a phone a little wider (the tiles stretch, the recents row shows more). At 320, in Slide Over, it is narrower than any phone the app was drawn for: the tab pill takes what the search circle leaves, the tiles' names drop a size.

Rotating or dragging the divider across 820 changes the layout under the same page and never remounts it: the song, the scroll, a half-typed search and an open sheet stay.

3 · Why

The width decides, never the device. Every width where the layout gives way is on one ruler, with what each side of it looks like. Controls stay finger-sized and visible, nothing waits for a hover, and there are no keyboard shortcuts on an iPad until a hardware keyboard is supported.

5 · Extension

The browser extension · same language, smaller

The popup is 360 wide. On a song: title and artist are editable before saving, and a tag can be added; never a playlist. On a playlist: click any name and both title and artist become fields; every song is coming in unless you click the far end of its row, which dims it and says 'Left out'. No checkmarks.

The other states, the pill on YouTube pages and the options page use the same surfaces, pills and type as the app.
